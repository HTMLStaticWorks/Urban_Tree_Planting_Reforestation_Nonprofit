How Our Nonprofit Works

From native seed to thriving canopy.

We operate an end-to-end reforestation framework that unites municipal planning, ecological science, local nurseries, and grassroots community stewardship.

Pillar 01

Site Assessment & GIS Mapping

We analyze urban heat islands, soil hydrology, and canopy deficits using GIS satellite data to identify neighborhoods in urgent need of green infrastructure.

Ecological Diagnostics
Pillar 02

Native Species & Nursery Sourcing

We propagate climate-resilient native saplings in partnership with regional nurseries, ensuring maximum biodiversity, disease resistance, and local ecosystem synergy.

Biodiverse Genetics
Pillar 03

Community-Led Planting Days

We organize hands-on community planting events, training local volunteer Tree Captains with professional arboricultural methods to plant every tree properly.

Grassroots Stewardship
Pillar 04

3-Year Monitored Aftercare

Unlike one-off planting drives, we maintain, irrigate, mulch, and monitor every sapling for a minimum of 36 months to guarantee a 90%+ survival rate into maturity.

Long-Term Survival
The 5-Step Process

How we plant and maintain every tree.

Proper tree establishment requires rigorous arboricultural care from soil preparation through multi-year juvenile monitoring.

01
Step 1 • Planning

Site Survey & Species Matching

Soil tests for compaction, pH, and percolation. We map utility lines and match native canopy species specifically to moisture levels and solar aspect.

✔ Deep soil diagnostic
02
Step 2 • Preparation

Excavation & Root Flare Positioning

Holes are dug 2 to 3 times the width of root balls with sloping sides. The tree's root flare is aligned exactly 1–2 inches above grade to avoid rot.

✔ Zero root girdling
03
Step 3 • Installation

Native Backfill & Micro-Tamping

We backfill with native unamended soil to prevent water basin pooling, gently tamping layers to remove air pockets without damaging fragile root hairs.

✔ Aerated root bed
04
Step 4 • Protection

Organic Mulch Ring & Slow Hydration

A 3-inch blanket of aged woodchips is applied in a protective donut shape, keeping mulch off the trunk bark. A 15-gallon slow-drip bag is installed.

✔ Moisture conservation
05
Step 5 • Stewardship

3-Year Monitoring & Growth Audits

Weekly watering through summer, seasonal structural pruning, collar adjustments, and biometric GPS logging verify sapling vitality into adult maturity.

✔ 92.4% verified survival

Real Impact Stories

Restoration success stories from the field.

Explore how dedicated neighbors, civic planners, and donors transformed degraded urban lots and barren waterways into thriving, biodiverse forest canopies.

Urban Heat Island
📍 Oakland, CA • Southside Transit & School Corridor

From Scorched Asphalt to Shaded Schoolyards

14 high-density asphalt zones that previously experienced extreme 108°F surface temperatures were reforested with 4,850 Coast Live Oaks and Western Redbuds.

"Our schoolyard used to be an unbearable asphalt oven by noon. Today, the students learn beneath healthy oak canopies they planted themselves." — Marcus Vance, Elementary School Principal & Volunteer
🌡️ -5.2°F Surface Cooling 🌲 91.5% Survival
Riparian Recovery
📍 Portland, OR • Willamette River Basin

Reviving Salmon Habitats along 4.2 Miles of Riverbank

Re-established native riparian buffer zones with 15,200 Pacific Willows and Bigleaf Maples, stabilizing severely eroded riverbanks and cooling water temperatures.

"The canopy shade lowered stream temperatures by 2.1°F, allowing juvenile Chinook salmon to return to shallows they had abandoned for a decade." — Dr. Elena Chavez, Riparian Fisheries Biologist
🐟 78% Bank Erosion Halt 🌲 94.2% Survival
Miyawaki Micro-Forest
📍 Austin, TX • East End Community Park

An Ultra-Dense Urban Micro-Forest in 18 Months

Transformed a flood-prone, compacted 1.5-acre municipal lot into a 30-species Miyawaki forest that grows 10x faster and absorbs 120,000+ gallons of stormwater runoff.

"In just 18 months, our bare dusty lot became a lush, closed-canopy bird sanctuary that absorbs torrential flash-flood rains naturally." — Sofia Morales, East End Community Tree Captain
💧 120k Gal Storm Absorption 🌲 95.8% Survival
